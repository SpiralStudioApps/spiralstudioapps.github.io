How to set app time limits on Android (that survive a restart)

Spiral Studio · October 2026

A daily limit is the gentlest kind of block. You don't give up YouTube, you give it an hour. The trouble is that most limits on Android are easy to get around: one tap to ignore, a timer you can delete, or a counter that quietly goes back to zero when the phone restarts. Here's what Android offers, and what a limit needs to actually hold.

1. Digital Wellbeing app timers (built in)

Settings → Digital Wellbeing & parental controls → Dashboard, tap the app, then App timer. Pick a daily amount, say 30 minutes of Instagram. When it runs out, the icon turns grey and the app is paused until midnight.

Good: free, built in, and Android itself counts the time. Not so good: the timer lives one screen away from where you hit it. When the app is paused, you can go straight to its settings and delete the timer, and on the evenings you most need the limit, that's exactly what happens.

2. The apps' own reminders

YouTube (Settings → General → "Remind me to take a break") and Instagram (Your activity → Time spent) can nudge you after a set time. They're reminders, not limits: one tap closes them and you keep watching. Useful as a gentle signal, not as a boundary.

3. Family Link

Google Family Link lets a parent set daily limits on a child's phone that the child can't remove. It works well for what it's for. For your own phone it doesn't fit: you'd be your own parent, holding your own PIN.

4. What a daily limit needs to actually hold

If the built-in timers are too easy to delete, a dedicated app can enforce the limit. Before you trust one, check these:

  • It survives a restart. Some apps count the minutes themselves, so a restart (or Android closing the app in the background) resets the count. A limit should read the time from Android's own usage records. Then a restart changes nothing, because Android kept counting.
  • It comes back on after a reboot. The blocking itself has to start again when the phone boots, or the first restart ends the limit for the day.
  • It can't be loosened on impulse. If you can raise the limit or delete it the moment it runs out, it's the Digital Wellbeing timer again.
  • It warns you first. A few minutes' notice lets you finish the video instead of being cut off mid-sentence.
  • The day resets at a sensible time. If the day ends at midnight, the 12:30 am scroll counts as a fresh day.

Daily Limits in Stillness

In Stillness, a daily limit gives an app its time, an hour of YouTube for example. When it's spent, the app rests until tomorrow. A quiet note comes before the time runs out (five minutes before, for limits of half an hour or more).

  • Restarts don't reset it. Stillness reads the time from Android's own usage records, on your device, so the count is the same after a reboot. The limit comes back on by itself.
  • Strict mode holds the day's rest in place: once an app is resting, it can't be woken or have its limit raised until the reset.
  • The day resets at 4:00 am, not midnight, so late-night scrolling still counts against today.
