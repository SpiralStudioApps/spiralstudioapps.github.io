How to block distracting apps on Android: 4 ways compared

Spiral Studio · Updated October 2026

You open an app without deciding to, and twenty minutes disappear. Blocking the app is the obvious fix, but "block" can mean very different things on Android. Here are four approaches, from softest to strictest.

ApproachWhat happensHow easy to undo
App timersApp pauses after its daily timeEdit the timer in Settings
Focus modeChosen apps paused while it's on"Take a break" button
Session blockChosen apps blocked for a set timeDepends on the app
Daily limit + strict modeApp rests once its time is spentCan't loosen it while the app is resting

1. App timers (Digital Wellbeing)

Settings → Digital Wellbeing → Dashboard → tap an app → App timer. When the time is up, the app is paused for the rest of the day.

Good: free and built in. Not so good: you can raise or delete the timer from the same screen whenever you're tempted.

2. Focus mode

Choose a set of apps and pause them all at once, on demand or on a schedule. It works well for work hours. It's designed to be easy to switch off, though, so it relies on your willpower in the moment.

3. Block apps for a session

Third-party blockers can block a list of apps for a set length of time, say two hours of deep work, while the rest of the phone keeps working. The important questions are whether the block survives a restart and whether you can end it early.

In Stillness this is Selective Lock: pick the apps that pull you in, set a duration, and hold to start. Maps, music and messages keep working, and the blocked apps stay closed until the time is up.

4. Daily limits that hold

A daily limit suits apps you don't want to quit entirely, like an hour of YouTube a day. Two details decide whether it works:

  • Who counts the time. If Android itself counts usage (Usage Access), restarting the phone can't reset it.
  • Whether you can loosen it in the moment. The urge always arrives once the time's up.

Stillness's Daily Limits rest an app until 4 am once its time is spent. You get a gentle heads-up a few minutes before. With the optional strict mode on, you can't pause, remove or raise a limit while that app is resting.

A practical setup that works for most people: a daily limit on your one or two worst apps, a scheduled Full Lock overnight, and a Selective Lock when you sit down to work. That's three small rules, and you don't have to fight yourself all day.
