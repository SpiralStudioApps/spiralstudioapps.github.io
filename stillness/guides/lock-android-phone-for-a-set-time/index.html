How to lock your Android phone for a set time (and make it stick)

Spiral Studio · Updated October 2026

Sometimes you don't want to use your phone less. You want it gone for the next two hours: for an exam, a deep-work block, a dinner, or a night's sleep. Android has a few tools that get close. This guide covers what each one actually does and where it falls short.

1. Focus mode (Digital Wellbeing)

Settings → Digital Wellbeing & parental controls → Focus mode. Pick the apps that distract you and turn it on, now or on a schedule. Those apps are paused: their icons go grey and their notifications stop.

Where it falls short: it's built to be gentle. There's a "Take a break" button right in the notification, and you can switch Focus mode off in two taps. The rest of the phone stays fully usable.

2. Bedtime mode

Also in Digital Wellbeing. At a set time the screen turns greyscale and Do Not Disturb comes on. It's surprisingly effective at making the phone boring, but it doesn't stop you using anything.

3. Your manufacturer's own mode

Some brands ship a stronger lock. OnePlus has Zen Mode, and realme and some Oppo phones have similar focus modes that hide apps for a fixed period. If your phone has one, it's worth trying. They're limited to those brands, though, and usually to short sessions.

4. App pinning (screen pinning)

Settings → Security → App pinning lets you lock the phone to a single app. It's meant for handing your phone to someone else, and it unpins with a button combination, so it isn't a timer.

Why the built-in options are easy to undo

A normal Android app can't lock your whole phone. Doing that needs Android's Device Admin permission, and keeping the lock in place usually also needs Accessibility access, so the app can tell when you've opened something else. That's why a real timed lock is always a separate app you grant those permissions to.

The other weak point is restarting. Many lock apps simply stop when the phone reboots, so "turn it off and on again" becomes the escape hatch.

What to look for in a lock app
  • A fixed duration you can't shorten once it starts.
  • It comes back after a restart.
  • Calls and emergency calls still work. You shouldn't be cut off from people who need you.
  • Permissions used only for locking, and no data collection.

One tip for Xiaomi, Huawei and OnePlus phones

These brands aggressively close background apps to save battery, which can interfere with any lock or timer app. If a lock ever slips, open your battery settings and allow the app to run in the background (on Xiaomi: App info → Battery saver → No restrictions, and turn on Autostart).
